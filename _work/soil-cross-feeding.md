This paper is the through-line for a lot of my current work and the most direct signal of where I want to go next: building tools that help agbio companies design SynComs rationally rather than by trial-and-error.

The core question was simple. In a complex soil community, who depends on whom? "Obligate" cross-feeding — where strain B can't grow without a metabolite secreted by strain A — is the kind of dependency that breaks SynCom products in the field even after they pass lab QC. We built a framework that predicts these dependencies directly from genome-scale metabolic models and the pangenomic structure of the community.

For an agtech company building bioaugmentation products, this is a pre-launch screen. For a researcher trying to understand soil health, it's a knob to turn. For me, it's the bridge between the computational tooling I've spent seven years building and the industrial questions I want to keep working on.
